### Background
Jules is **24**, a recent graduate with a communications degree and **no prior content-moderation experience** — a different entry path from Maya's community-support pivot or Theo's long trust & safety career. Jules is **three weeks into the role**, still functionally in an onboarding period, but per the platform's confirmed case-assignment workflow, is already receiving live, weighted-assigned cases like any other auditor — there is no confirmed system-level distinction between a new hire's queue and an experienced auditor's queue.

Jules represents the Auditor role's **earliest-tenure** variant, positioned before Maya (3 years) on the same calibration spectrum that runs through to Theo (9 years). The meaningful difference from Maya is not just degree of experience but *kind*: Maya has enough tenure to have formed some working relationship with the wellbeing systems, even if it's still uncertain; Jules has none yet, and is forming first impressions of whether declining, SOS, and cooldowns are genuinely safe to use, purely from what training material and early-days observation tell them, not lived confidence. This variant is grounded directly in Marielle Lee's guidance from her expert interview: *"warn people before they're hired that the role involves viewing this kind of material — set expectations at the outset, not just at point-of-content."*

### Goals
- Understand what a "normal" reaction to this work actually looks like — Jules doesn't yet have a baseline to judge their own responses against.
- Get through onboarding without either under-reacting (accepting cases they're not ready for, to seem capable) or over-reacting (declining everything out of uncertainty).
- Learn, explicitly rather than by inference, that declining and SOS are legitimate, expected parts of the job from day one — not something earned after proving oneself.
- Build early trust that the platform's protections (blur-by-default, exposure cap, cooldown) are real safeguards, not just documented policy.

### Frustrations / Pain Points
Sourced primarily from Marielle Lee's expert interview, applied to the confirmed Auditor mechanics; a hypothesis, not a confirmed finding, and thinner grounding than Maya's or Theo's since no source document describes an onboarding-stage auditor directly.

1. **Insufficient expectation-setting before first exposure.** This is Jules's dominant pain point, drawn directly from Marielle Lee's guidance. No confirmed onboarding flow exists inside the platform itself — the source material is silent on this entirely. If real expectation-setting happens (or doesn't) purely outside the system, Jules's first real understanding of the job's weight may come from the content itself, not from being told beforehand.
2. **No lived track record with the wellbeing systems yet.** Where Maya's uncertainty about decline-stigma comes from having used the option and wondering whether it counted against her, Jules's version of the same uncertainty is more basic: not yet knowing from any real experience whether these protections actually work as described.
3. **Limited self-calibration to notice early warning signs.** Without Theo's years of pattern-recognition, Jules may not recognise that a case is affecting them until it has already become a problem — a plausible but unconfirmed risk given the role's confirmed cumulative-exposure model.
4. **No confirmed onboarding or training flow inside the platform.** This is an open gap this persona surfaces, not something the client has ruled on either way — worth raising with PM/BA as a scope question, not assuming it's out of scope.

### Tasks (Touchpoints)
1. Log in and view assigned case list — thumbnails auto-blurred/suppressed by default, severity-tagged, with no confirmed distinction shown for a new hire's queue.
2. Open a case: review AI severity score, incident timeline, narrative summary, and audio analysis, likely more slowly and carefully than a tenured reviewer.
3. Pass through the consent/warning gate before viewing raw flagged content — plausibly with more hesitation than either Maya or Theo, given no prior lived experience with the system's protections.
4. Toggle blur/grayscale to self-control exposure level while reviewing, while still learning what level of exposure feels manageable.
5. Adjust the AI's severity/CVI rating if confident enough to disagree, write comments, and submit findings to the manager.
6. Trigger SOS if unexpectedly exposed, or use a wellbeing check-in action — the first time doing so with no prior experience of what happens next.

### Context of Use
Structurally identical working context to Maya and Theo (same shift-based queue, same cumulative exposure model, same desktop-based work setting), but experienced very differently: every mechanism (consent gate, blur toggle, decline, SOS) is being encountered for the first time rather than relied upon from habit. As a team-defined characteristic of this scenario (not a confirmed system feature), we assume Jules receives some form of orientation before starting live case review, even though the platform itself has no confirmed onboarding flow.

### Constraints
- **Daily exposure cap: 2 hours / 120 minutes** — same client-confirmed target as the other Auditor personas.
- **Case assignment is weighted**, not purely random, by exposure level and case count, per client confirmation; there is no confirmed system distinction reducing a new hire's exposure relative to a tenured auditor.
- **Wellbeing always outranks moderation speed** — the same governing client ruling applies to Jules as to every auditor, arguably most consequentially given the lack of self-calibration.
- **Role-based access**: Jules sees only their own assigned cases, same as every auditor.
- **Mandatory cooldown** enforced after high-severity content exposure, regardless of tenure.
- **Right to decline exists, but is a lower-build-priority feature** — same as the other Auditor personas; Jules's decline experience is unaffected by tenure at the system level.
